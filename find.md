# Find (quickselect)

In the night, when the stars are bright, and when the moon looms over the trees, a squirrel approached her brother and told him that she discovered a new algorithm, an algorithm called Find.

"What's find?" her brother, a squirrel, asked.

"It's an algorithm for finding the kth smallest element in an unsorted list," said his sister.

Her brother scratched his head, in a way that only a squirrel could do.

"Does it have an application?" asked her brother.

"What's the application?" he asked again.

"What's the application?" his sister repeated after him.

"WTA," said her brother.

His sister was ready for this question.

"It can be used to find the median of an unsorted list. It can also be used to find the quartiles," she said.

Her brother was impressed.

"Wow, that's an application," he said.

"It's a really nice application," his sister said.

"But is it as useful as quicksort?" her brother asked.

"It was invented by the same man who invented quicksort, Tony Hoare," his sister replied.

Her brother thought about this for a while.

"Well, if it was created by the inventor of quicksort, then I should learn it," he said.

"It's also called quickselect," his sister explained.

The two squirrels, brother and sister, stayed up late, talking about Quicksort and Find, which is also called Quickselect.

They were young - young enough to be children.

And when their parents returned to the tree where the brother and sister were sitting, they notified their children that it was well past their bedtime, and it was not appropriate for a squirrel of their age to be up so late.

(It was a heady debate, in the population of squirrels, what the bedtime of a minor should be.)

"But I haven't finished learning the Find algorithm!" said the son, also known as the brother.

"Why don't you find time to learn it tomorrow," said his mother, gently.

With a whisk of her tail, the mother squirrel sent the two children to bed.

(They knew what that means... when mom whisks her tail like that... it means she's serious.)

As the two children slept, brother and sister, on an oak tree, in the forest, mom and dad stayed up late providing them with security.

It wasn't until Owl arrived, the guardian owl of the forest, that mom and dad went to bed, themselves.

"What's this?" asked Owl. "Why did they stay up so late?" Owl asked mom and dad.

"They were talking about an algorithm called Find, by Tony Hoare," said the mom.

"It's also called Quickselect," said the dad.

"That's a nice algorithm," said Owl. "I learned that when I was two."

"You learned that when you were two?" asked mom.

"When I was two," said Owl.

The dad scratched his head.

"None of us know the algorithm yet," he said. "But we do know quicksort."

"Well learn it," said Owl. "During the day, when you are well rested."

And with that, mom and dad went to bed, next to their children, while the guardian owl of the forest looked over them.
